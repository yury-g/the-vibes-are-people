The project studies visible algorithmic methods and their public histories. It cannot reconstruct a model's private training dataset or prove influence from appearance alone. Resource links imply neither endorsement nor a training-data inclusion claim.

## A vocabulary for making algorithmic art

The footer pairs the existing skill’s capabilities with our suggested teaching workflow: observable quality → named method → adjustable control → specific, sourced contribution. A worked brief uses Perlin-noise flow fields and fixed-seed comparisons. It links to the interactive vocabulary so artists, students, teachers and their agents can develop and test more precise requests.

The OpenAI image-generation card was removed to keep this resource focused on algorithmic art. This is an editorial focus, not a claim that the Anthropic skill lacks vocabulary or that an improvement in outputs has been experimentally measured. The external skill runs in the visitor’s own tool; the site supplies exercises and research. Established method names and credited or chosen person names preserve attribution without assigning a whole aesthetic to one inventor.
